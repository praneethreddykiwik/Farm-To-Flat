01

The promise

Why anyone would pay more for a tomato

Urban households have almost no visibility into how their food is grown, fed, stored and handled. Price does not fix this. A restaurant charging ₹400–₹500 a dish can still run on aged stock, because the customer never sees the kitchen and rarely complains unless something visibly wrong turns up in the food. Where penalties land, they are absorbed as a cost of doing business.

Meat is the sharpest gap. Goat and sheep sold in the city is often bulk-procured from out of state, cage-reared and grown to maximum carcass weight — 20 kg where a naturally grown animal would be 15 kg. Commercial poultry is widely assumed to be chemically accelerated. Farmed fish raised in small confined ponds carries the same suspicion. Customers already believe village-reared meat is different, because it is.

Meanwhile behaviour has shifted. Health-conscious households have cut back or stopped ordering cooked food outside altogether, particularly through monsoon, and are actively hunting for fresh alternatives.

End of the day you are making money for your health.

Business Owner · discovery

What makes it work

A large gated community concentrates that demand into one delivery point: 1,000+ flats, 900+ occupied, all reachable in a single trip. The unit of profitability is not the order — it is the community. Ten orders inside one gate and the economics work.

Compliance note. Competitors advertise sourcing regions and organic credentials nobody can verify. Claim only what the batch records can substantiate — it protects the brand and satisfies food-safety traceability.

The aggregation engine

The one thing a spreadsheet cannot do

Small household orders roll up into one procurable quantity, then break back down again — by community, by block, by flat — with no re-keying anywhere in the chain.

I ordered ten kg. You order two kg. She ordered two kg… all that put together, today we have fifty kg tomato to procure.

05

Where the margin lives

Price is derived, never typed

Tomato moved from roughly ₹10 a kilo to nearly ₹100 within a season. A fixed price list cannot survive that. Procurement enters what the goods actually cost tonight; the system applies the configured margin and publishes tomorrow's price.

Tomatoes I will keep ten percent margin. Brinjal five percent. For meat thirty percent… And later I may think no, I need only three percent. So everything has to have the control to tweak that.

Margin resolves in precedence order — product, then category, then global default. Changes apply forward only; orders already placed keep the price they were confirmed at. Every change is versioned with who made it and when.

What the customer never seesnew

The original model priced against procurement cost alone and missed the consumables — covers, rubber bands, stickers, printed labels. Those are a real per-packet cost, and leaving them out overstated profit on every single order.

You are procuring tomato for ten rupees a kilo, putting ten percent margin… But how about the raw materials? The bags, the rubber bands, the stickering cost, the printing labels — everything is raw material for me.

Business Owner · 21 August review

Price is built from five components. Only two of them are ever shown to the customer.

Look at the arithmetic. A ₹1.00 consumables cost against a 10% margin on a ₹10 product wipes out the entire margin. The control panel has to surface that when the margin is set, not at month end.

Allocating it without anyone doing arithmetic

Admin records a bulk purchase — 100 covers for ₹100. The system derives ₹1.00 per cover and consumes it automatically as packets are produced. No packer, agent or admin ever types a per-order figure.

You should not give this job as a manual job to your admin or the delivery agents. The system should automatically take overall cost within our procurement.

Business Owner · 21 August review

What the owner sees

Reporting runs off the actual recorded cost, not the reference cost, so profit on screen is profit in reality. Fourteen reports now cover operations, cost, revenue, margin and P&L, wastage, delivery and SLA performance, community and product profitability, premium runs, consumables usage, customers and collections.

Aggregate profit was explicitly rejected as a management number on its own — the point is to be able to stop selling a product or stop serving a community on evidence.

I need to understand how much are we making on tomatoes, how much on meat products, how much for this community. That way we can decide we are not going to serve that community, or not going to sell this product.

06

Selling only what we can buynew

Stock thresholds — the exposure of procuring after you sell

This platform sells first and buys second, which creates a risk no ordinary grocery app carries: the system can accept an order the supply chain physically cannot fill. Twenty kilos of tomato is routine. Five hundred, because one community happened to order together, may simply not exist at the market that night.

Meat makes the ceiling physical rather than statistical. Promising fresh meat means buying live animals — two goats at roughly 15 kg each is 30 kg, and no setting changes that.

I planned a BBQ party. I invited everybody. Today night I ordered ten kg meat… and then it is not delivered. That is going to be brand failure. So instead I will not make any profit, I will not make the business — that would be good.

Business Owner · 21 August review

So admin sets a per-product ceiling for each fulfilment date. Orders eat into it first-come, first-served. At zero the product goes out of stock and cannot be added to a cart. Cancelling before cut-off puts the quantity back.

The governing trade. Refusing an order costs one sale. Accepting an order and failing to deliver costs the customer permanently.

Premium deliverynew

The standard model batches deliveries onto one optimised route. Some orders are worth breaking that route for — a 4 a.m. drop before a morning pooja, a fixed 7 a.m. slot — and customers will pay for it. The argument for offering it is not the uplift; it is that without the option the sale disappears entirely.

I am okay to pay extra ten rupees, no matter, because it is a demand. But did you give me that option? If you do not give me that option, I will not even consider that — I will go buy it myself.

Business Owner · 21 August review

Premium orders are excluded from consolidation and planned as direct runs, capped per slot and per day, flagged in every operational view so nobody batches them by mistake, and reported separately so the business can check the uplift actually covers the dedicated trip.

07

When a delivery runs latenew

The escalation ladder — identified as the competitive differentiator

This is the largest addition in the revision, and it exists to fix a failure everyone in the room had personally experienced with existing delivery apps: the order stalls, the tracker freezes on an optimistic number, nobody calls, and nobody picks up.

Two minutes was two minutes for at least ten minutes… nobody came to pick up. It just said the delivery partner is reaching soon, and they do not pick up the call.

Business Owner · 21 August review

The system detects the breach itself. No customer should have to ring to find out their order is late, and no admin should have to notice.

I do not want any manual intervention, where someone calls and only then we realise. The system can automatically say that. It knows.

Business Owner · 21 August review

Every threshold, recipient and message above is configurable. These are the defaults discussed, not fixed behaviour — the real values are OQ-21.

Three details carry most of the value. The agent gets a chance to fix it quietly at P4, with a capped snooze. The customer is told at P3 with a resolution window drawn from config, so the business never promises five minutes and misses. And escalation stops dead the moment delivery is confirmed.

Supportnew

Someone has to answer what the system now raises. In Phase 1 that is the admin, because in practice one person runs the operation — but the role, the queue and the routing exist from the start so it can become a team without rework.

Known tension. Support is expected to explain a delay, but live agent tracking sits in Phase 3. Without it, support can report status and elapsed time but not location. Whether that is enough for Phase 1 is OQ-19.

    How it gets built

    Business Owner · discovery

    Development starts at prototype sign-off, not requirement sign-off. Static mockups and AI-generated screen images are explicitly rejected: the deliverable is a clickable prototype where pressing something produces the next screen, using realistic data.

    What the prototype has to be

    The prototype covers every screen listed below, with realistic sample data — not lorem ipsum, not placeholder imagery. Its job is to eliminate interpretation risk cheaply: a misunderstanding caught in an HTML page costs hours, the same one caught in a built module costs weeks.

    Sequencing notes

    Build aggregation and pricing first — everything downstream reads their output. Spike the routing and slot-feasibility logic early; it carries the most technical uncertainty and is the worst thing to discover late. A single-community pilot is mandatory before rollout, because building access, packet handover and cash handling only get tested in reality.

      Release 1.021 August review

      Five product changes, plus the first pass at marketing and offers. The five are detailed in full earlier in this document; the marketing and promotion work below was drafted from that discussion and has since been overtaken in places by 2.0.

      A. How we get the first customers — SUPERSEDED IN PART BY 2.5

      The delivery model only works at ten orders inside one gate. So the question is not "how do we reach Hyderabad" — it is "how do we get ten households in one community ordering on the same day." That is a very different, and much cheaper, marketing problem.

      Four channels, in the order we would actually use them. The numbers assume a single community of roughly 1,000 flats with 900 occupied.

      The sequence we would run

      Not all four at once. Each does a different job, and running them in order costs less than running them together.

      Total to open one community: roughly ₹8,000. One stall at ₹5,000, one leaflet drop at ₹3,000, WhatsApp free. If that produces 45–70 first orders, we are paying about ₹120–₹180 to acquire a household. The second community costs the same, and the fifth costs the same — this does not get cheaper with scale, which is why the association partnership matters.

      B. What we offer to start — EXTENDED BY 2.6

      Five options were considered. They are not equal, and one of them would lose money on every order it brought in.

      Why the flat 15% would hurt us

      This is worth walking through, because a launch discount is the most natural thing in the world to offer and this one is not survivable. Take a typical ₹500 order:

      Fifty-three rupees per order is what the business actually earns after the food, the packaging and the van. Now apply each offer to that same order:

      A 15% discount is larger than the whole margin. At 20 orders a day for a month that is roughly ₹37,000 of real loss, and it sets a price the customer will expect again in month two. The free item and the free delivery buy nearly the same excitement and stay in profit.

      The reason the gap is so sharp: the margin on vegetables is thin by design — often five to ten percent — and the consumables now take a real bite out of that. Meat at thirty percent could carry a discount. Tomatoes cannot. If a percentage discount is wanted, it should apply to meat and fish only, never the whole catalog.
